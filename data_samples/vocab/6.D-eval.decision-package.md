# Issue #6 — decision package: what counts as a correct answer? (O2 / workstream D)

**For:** @motyc and @david-spacil (the calls), jointly — this is the D1 rubric.
**From:** the vocabulary tooling side — this document decides nothing, it makes each decision cheap and shows what it costs to defer.
**Data:** `data_samples/vocab/specificity_pairs.csv` (`python3 vocab_review.py --specificity`), `composite_pairs.csv`, `collision_review.csv`
**Instrument:** `python3 corpus_review.py --gold-workbook` already produces the per-line annotation sheet D2 needs.

Workstream D has been untouched since 11 August and is now the only thing between the
vocabulary and a trustworthy number. Every other question in this issue — O1/F, O3/O4,
M8 — is ultimately settled by *measuring* the alternatives. None of that measurement
means anything until the scoring rule is written down.

---

## The short version

**A strict exact-match score would call 44 % of the vocabulary's own answers wrong for
reasons that have nothing to do with the model.** Not because the model failed, but
because the vocabulary deliberately offers several defensible labels for the same line:

| Ambiguity                                         |                                                      Terms affected | Sheet                     | Decision |
|---------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------:|---------------------------|----------|
| **A term and its own broader term, both offered** | **914** of 1 297 TEATER terms (70 %) — 44 % of the whole vocabulary | `specificity_pairs.csv`   | **D1-a** |
| **A composite `X/Y` and its components**          |                                       **162** terms in **98** pairs | `composite_pairs.csv`     | **D1-b** |
| **One label, several source ids**                 |                                  **470** terms (23 %) carry 2–4 ids | `collision_review.csv`    | **D1-c** |
| **A bracketed homonym vs its bare label**         |         1 today (`zámek (sídlo elity)`), by construction more later | `taxonomy_overrides.json` | **D1-d** |

None of these is a bug. Each is a decision already taken and already defended in this
thread — M7 keeps every absorbed id, P1 keeps both sides of a composite, M8 splits a
confirmed homonym, and TEATER's `broader` hierarchy is the granularity the sources
curate. What is missing is the other half: **what the scorer does when the model picks
the sibling of the gold answer.**

## D1-a — partial credit for a less-specific term (914 terms)

This is the big one and it was previously assumed to be a corner case. It is not.

TEATER curates a real `broader` hierarchy and the facet rollup keeps *both* tiers, so
70 % of the TEATER side of the vocabulary is offered directly underneath an ancestor
the model could equally have chosen:

| Term                           | ⊂ nearest offered ancestor   | … up to                 | Rungs |
|--------------------------------|------------------------------|-------------------------|------:|
| `paleogén`                     | `třetihory`                  | `geologická doba`       |     3 |
| `digitální model terénu`       | `letecké laserové skenování` | `archeologické výzkumy` |     5 |
| `atlantik (paleoklimatologie)` | `holocén`                    | `geologická doba`       |     4 |

Distribution of how many offered ancestors sit above a term:

| Rungs above |   1 |   2 |  3 |  4 | 5 |
|-------------|----:|----:|---:|---:|--:|
| Terms       | 536 | 282 | 74 | 21 | 1 |

**Under strict exact match, a model answering `třetihory` where the gold label is
`paleogén` scores exactly what a model answering `keramika` scores: zero.** The metric
cannot tell a one-rung near miss from a category error, and 914 terms are exposed to
that.

**The escape hatch does not exist.** Scoring the facet instead of the term would not
help: **every one of the 914 pairs sits inside a single facet** (asserted by
`test_every_specificity_pair_sits_inside_one_facet`). Facet-level scoring gives the
near miss and the exact hit the same score too — in the other direction. The rule has
to be made at term level.

**Options** (each is a scorer parameter, not a vocabulary change):

1. **Exact only.** Simple, defensible, and reports a number lower than the model's real
   performance by an amount nobody can estimate without doing (2) anyway.
2. **Credit any ancestor, at a fixed weight** (e.g. 0.5). One rule, no tuning.
3. **Decay by rungs** (e.g. `0.5 ** rungs_above`). `třetihory` for `paleogén` scores
   0.5; `geologická doba` for `paleogén` scores 0.125.
4. **Credit only one rung up.** The narrowest reading of "nearly right".

Report both the strict and the credited number regardless of which is chosen — the gap
between them is itself the useful diagnostic, and computing both costs nothing once the
ancestor map exists (it is `specificity_pairs.csv`).

**The sheet has a blank `verdict` column** for per-row rulings, but per-row is almost
certainly the wrong grain here: 914 rows is not an afternoon. The column exists so that
a handful of pairs that are *genuinely* different concepts rather than a specificity
ladder can be marked as such.

## D1-b — either side of a composite pair (162 terms, 98 pairs)

Already decided in substance: both sides stay selectable, linked by `same_as`, and P1
forbids dropping either. The open half is the scoring rule.

- 84 pairs sit inside one facet; 14 cross two facets (`budova/stavba` in Feature ↔
  `budova` in Finds Context ↔ `stavba` in Methods).
- `most/brod` ↔ `most`, `muzeum/skanzen` ↔ `muzeum` + `skanzen`,
  `zámek/zámeček/vila` ↔ `vila`.

**Proposed rule, for confirmation rather than debate: either side counts correct.**
The links are already in the built vocabulary as `same_as` on both entries, so the
scorer reads them rather than recomputing anything. The 14 cross-facet pairs are the
reason this must be a term-level rule and not a facet-level one.

If a pair is judged *not* equivalent, that is a `same_as_suppress` entry in
`taxonomy_overrides.json` — one line, no code — and the sheet's `link_status` column
then shows it as `suppressed`.

## D1-c — is `teater_category_ids` scored, or only the label? (470 terms)

M7 shipped `discarded_ids`, and `EMIT_CATEGORY_IDS=true` puts the full id set on every
output record. 470 of 2 074 offered labels (23 %) stand for more than one source
record — 2 657 ids behind 2 074 labels:

| Ids behind one label |    1 |   2 |   3 | 4 |
|----------------------|-----:|----:|----:|--:|
| Terms                | 1604 | 363 | 101 | 6 |

The model only ever selects a **label**; the ids are attached afterwards by
`_attach_category_ids`. So the question is not "can the model get the id right" — it
cannot get it wrong — but **whether the annotator records an id at all**.

- **Label only** (recommended default): the gold workbook's `gold_category` is a Czech
  label, annotation stays fast, and the ids are provenance rather than a scored field.
- **Label + id**: forces the annotator to disambiguate which of 2–4 records they meant,
  on 23 % of lines. That is real annotation cost, and it buys a distinction the model
  was never asked to make.

Choosing "label only" does not throw the ids away — they stay on the output record for
downstream linking, which is what M7 asked for.

## D1-d — bracketed homonyms vs the bare label

`zámek (sídlo elity)` is the only split today, but M8's mechanism means more will
follow from `collision_review.csv` (127 ranked groups, ~30 worth reading).

Each split entry carries `bare_cs` — the unqualified label — precisely so the bracket
can be stripped after inference. So the scorer *can* be lenient at no cost.

**The question:** does a gold annotation of `zámek` count as correct when the model
answers `zámek (sídlo elity)`? Strictly it should not — the split exists because the
two are different concepts, and accepting the bare form throws away exactly the
distinction M8 was created to capture. But a human annotator handed a line about a
château may well write `zámek`, and the annotation guidelines have to say which.

**Cheapest resolution:** the gold workbook offers the *qualified* labels in its
`candidate_terms` column, so the annotator picks a bracketed form rather than typing a
bare one. Then strict matching is correct and no rule is needed.

## D1-e — the other two scored fields

The output record carries four fields (`llm_run.py`):

```json
{"extracted_keywords_cs": [...], "extracted_keywords_en": [...],
 "teater_category": "kostel", "confidence_score": 0.92}
```

- **`teater_category`** is a single choice from a closed enum — accuracy, per facet and
  overall.
- **`extracted_keywords_cs` / `_en`** are open free-text multisets — micro P/R/F1 over
  `(text)` tuples after NFC + casefold + whitespace normalisation.

These are different measurements and **should be reported separately**, not folded into
one headline number. `extracted_keywords_en` is a translation of `_cs` by construction,
so scoring both double-counts; propose scoring `_cs` and reporting `_en` agreement as a
consistency check.

**`Nerelevantní (meta-text)`** is not a vocabulary term — it is added at schema level as
the fallback. It needs its own cell in the confusion matrix, because the two failure
directions cost differently: a real archaeological line labelled meta-text is silent
data loss; a header labelled `kostel` is visible noise.

## D2 — ⚠ blocked, and not on us

**There is no evaluation corpus in this repository.**

- `data_samples/DOC_LINE_CATEG/` holds **3 synthetic files, 16 data rows** — not the
  618 lines the thread's numbers assume.
- `README.md` links model output sets on **21 lines** (45 mentions, **23 distinct
  `KW_PER_DOC_LLM_*` directory names**, including a `data_samples/archived_KW_PER_DOC_LLM/`
  tree). **None of them is tracked in git, and the directory does not exist on disk.**
- The 16 real CTX documents arrived as a **zip attachment on issue #19** and were never
  committed.

This has stayed invisible because the README implies the data is there. The instrument
is ready — `corpus_review.py --gold-workbook` writes `file_id, page, line,
original_text, categ, quality_score, model_category, model_keywords_cs,
candidate_terms, gold_category, gold_keywords_cs, annotator_note` — and
`corpus_review.py` prints its own corpus size before writing anything, precisely so a
figure computed over three placeholders can never be mistaken for one computed over
real reports.

**To unblock:** commit the issue #19 corpus, and re-run at least one model to produce
the `KW_PER_DOC_LLM_*` set the workbook's `model_category` column reads. Nothing else
in D depends on a decision — only on that data.

## What each answer costs

| Decision | If answered                                            | If deferred                                                                      |
|----------|--------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **D1-a** | one scorer parameter; both numbers reported either way | every score understates the model by an unknown amount across 914 terms          |
| **D1-b** | confirm one sentence; the links are already built      | 98 pairs scored as errors, and O1/F reopens                                      |
| **D1-c** | one column in the annotation guidelines                | annotators guess, inconsistently, on 23 % of lines                               |
| **D1-d** | one sentence in the guidelines                         | M8's homonym splits are penalised by the metric that is supposed to justify them |
| **D1-e** | agree the reporting shape                              | one headline number that nobody can act on                                       |
| **D2**   | commit the issue #19 corpus                            | **everything above stays theoretical** — this is the real critical path          |

D1-b and D1-d are close to confirmations rather than decisions. D1-a is the one that
needs real thought, and it is the one with a measured sheet to think against.

## What this document does not do

- It does not choose a scoring rule. That is M10's division of labour: the tooling side
  measures, the domain side rules.
- It does not annotate anything. D2 is blocked on data, not on effort.
- It does not touch the vocabulary. Every option above is a scorer parameter or a line
  in the annotation guidelines; none of them changes what the prompt offers.
